Bookkeeping & PayrollFor small businesses in Edmonton, Sherwood Park & Strathcona County

Monthly or quarterly bookkeeping, payroll and GST filings for Edmonton-area small businesses — so your books are reconciled, your remittances go to the CRA on time, and year-end is a short job instead of a scramble.

At a Glance

Bookkeeping & Payroll in Brief

Who it’s for
Sole proprietors, corporations, trades, clinics, restaurants and other small businesses with or without employees.
What’s included
Bookkeeping and reconciliations, payroll with CPP, EI and tax deductions, T4 slips, Records of Employment and GST returns.
Key deadlines
Payroll remittances are generally due by the 15th of the following month; T4 slips by the last day of February.
How to start
Book a free consultation. We review your current records and quote a monthly fee.

Key Dates

Payroll & GST Deadlines

Typical deadlines for small employers and GST registrants. Your remitter type and GST reporting period can change these dates.

WhenWhat is due
15th of each monthPayroll source deductions for the previous month, for most regular remitters.
Last day of FebruaryT4 and T4A slips and summaries to the CRA and to employees and contractors.
1 month after period endGST return and payment for monthly and quarterly filers.
3 months after year-endAnnual GST return for annual filers with a corporation (sole proprietors with a December 31 year-end generally file by June 15 but pay by April 30).
6 yearsHow long you generally must keep your books and records for the CRA.

What we need to get started

  • Access to your bank and credit card statements
  • Your bookkeeping software login, or permission to set one up
  • Your CRA business number and program accounts
  • Employee details, pay rates and TD1 forms (for payroll)
  • Sales invoices and purchase receipts
  • Last year-end financial statements or tax return
  • Loan, lease and vehicle agreements
  • Any recent CRA notices or letters

Frequently Asked Questions

Bookkeeping & Payroll Questions

Most small employers are regular remitters and must send the CRA the CPP, EI and income tax they deducted by the 15th of the month after the pay date. Larger employers may have to remit more often; the CRA tells you your remitter type.

Most businesses must register once their taxable sales exceed $30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters. Below that you are a small supplier and can choose to register voluntarily — which can make sense if you want to claim input tax credits on your costs.
