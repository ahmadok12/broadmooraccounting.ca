Corporate Tax ReturnsT2 & Alberta AT1 returns for Edmonton-area corporations

We prepare federal T2 and Alberta AT1 corporate income tax returns, year-end financial statements, and the slips that go with them for small and owner-managed corporations in Sherwood Park, Edmonton and across Alberta — and help owners plan how to pay themselves.

At a Glance

Corporate Tax Returns in Brief

Who it’s for
Canadian-controlled private corporations, holding companies, incorporated professionals and trades, and newly incorporated businesses.
What’s included
T2 and Alberta AT1 returns, year-end financial statements from your records, T4/T5 slips, and instalment calculations.
Deadlines
Returns are due 6 months after your year-end. Any balance is due 2 or 3 months after year-end.
2026 rates
Combined federal + Alberta rate of 11% on the first $500,000 of active business income for qualifying CCPCs; 23% general rate.

Key Dates

Corporate Tax Deadlines

Corporate deadlines are tied to your fiscal year-end, not the calendar year. For example, a December 31 year-end means a June 30 filing deadline.

WhenWhat is due
Monthly or quarterlyInstalment payments, if your corporation’s tax payable exceeds the CRA threshold.
2 months after year-endBalance of tax due for most corporations.
3 months after year-endBalance due for many Canadian-controlled private corporations that claimed the small business deduction (conditions apply).
6 months after year-endT2 return due to the CRA and AT1 return due to Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration.
Last day of FebruaryT4 and T5 slips for salaries and dividends paid in the previous calendar year.

Rates shown are the 2026 combined federal and Alberta corporate income tax rates. The small business rate applies only to active business income of qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations, and can be reduced by passive investment income.

What to bring

  • Bank and credit card statements to your year-end
  • Your bookkeeping file (QuickBooks Online or similar) or records
  • Sales and purchase invoices, and loan statements
  • Payroll records and any T4/T5 slips issued
  • Last year’s T2, AT1 and financial statements (new clients)
  • Articles of incorporation and share register
  • Asset purchases and sales during the year
  • CRA and Alberta TRA notices received

Corporate Tax Questions

The T2 return is due six months after the end of your corporation’s tax year — for a December 31 year-end, that is June 30. Any balance of tax is usually due two months after year-end, or three months for many Canadian-controlled private corporations that claim the small business deduction.

Generally yes. Alberta collects its own corporate income tax, so corporations with a permanent establishment in Alberta file an AT1 with Alberta Tax and Revenue Administration in addition to the federal T2. Some corporations qualify for an AT1 filing exemption; we confirm this for you each year.

For 2026, qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporations pay a combined federal and Alberta rate of 11% (9% federal plus 2% Alberta) on up to $500,000 of active business income. Income above that, or income that does not qualify, is taxed at the general combined rate of 23%.
